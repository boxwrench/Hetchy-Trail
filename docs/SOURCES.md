# Sources

Public, citable sources for the historical claims in `content/cards/`.

Every card's `## Source note` cites a key from this file. Before this register
existed, all 26 cards cited only `docs/Hetchy Trail Historical Game Mapping.pdf`
— an internal working brief with no public locator. That was adequate for
building the deck and inadequate for shipping it from a public agency.

**The internal brief is still cited**, as the design mapping. It records how a
historical fact became a card. This register records where the fact itself can
be checked by someone outside the project.

## How to read a card citation

```
SFPUC 2005 history, "San Joaquin Pipelines", p. 38 [SFPUC-2005].
Design mapping: docs/Hetchy Trail Historical Game Mapping.pdf, section 5.
```

Page numbers are the **printed page numbers of the publication**, which is what
a reader or historian will cite back. In the PDF linked below, the printed page
sits 4 pages later than the PDF page counter (printed p. 38 = PDF p. 42).

## Verification status

Each entry records what was actually checked, and when.

- **Verified** — the document was retrieved and its text read; the cited claim
  appears there.
- **Document verified, contents not machine-readable** — the URL resolves and
  returns the expected document, but its text could not be extracted, so the
  specific claim rests on secondary report rather than direct reading.
- **Not yet verified** — cited in a secondary source; not independently checked.

History of the Municipal Water Department & Hetch Hetchy System*, 2005 edition.
Editors' preface dated June 2005. 61 PDF pages.

<https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/about-us/HH_historybook-2005.pdf>

**Status: Verified.** Retrieved and full text extracted 2026-07-28. Title,
publisher and 2005 date confirmed from the document's own title page and
preface.

This is the agency's own published history. It is the right primary citation for
a piece shipping from SFPUC, and it supports the great majority of the deck.

## [NPS-YOSE] — Yosemite National Park, railroad and dam context

National Park Service, "Encroaching Civilization — Dams and Trains", Yosemite
National Park.

<https://www.nps.gov/yose/learn/historyculture/encroaching-civilization-dams-and-trains.htm>

**Status: Verified.** Retrieved and read 2026-07-28. Confirms the Hetch Hetchy
Railroad connected the valley to Groveland, 25 miles west, "to move men and
materials year-round"; O'Shaughnessy Dam construction began 1919; first water
reached San Francisco in 1934.

A useful second, non-agency voice on the railroad's year-round role.

## [NR-ENG6] — Hetch Hetchy Railroad locomotive

National Park Service, *National Register of Historic Places Inventory —
Nomination Form*, "Engine (Locomotive) No. 6, Hetch Hetchy Railroad, later of
the Pickering Lumber Company (Shay No. 6)", El Portal, California. Form No.
10-306 (Rev. 10-74).

<https://npshistory.com/publications/yose/nr-hetch-hetchy-rr-eng-6.pdf>

**Status: Verified.** Retrieved and text extracted 2026-07-28; document identity
and subject confirmed from its own header.

Relevant to the railroad's cargo and motive power. Cited in external research as
documenting cement trains on Priest Hill and a fuel-oil tank — **those specific
details have not been read out of the document here** and are marked as pending
in the claim register.

<https://webbie1.sfpl.org/multimedia/pdf/elections/May3_1932.pdf>

**Status: Document verified, contents not machine-readable.** The URL resolves

## Composite and general-practice claims

Five hazard cards (`h1`–`h5`) do not describe specific documented incidents.
They describe conditions that recurred across the project's two decades —
drill-and-blast danger, falls of ground, squeezing ground, political impatience,
ratepayer fatigue.

These carry no single-source citation because none is honest. They are
**composite** claims, and their source notes say so. If the historian wants any
of them tied to a specific documented event, that is a content change, not a
citation change.

## Open items for the historian

Claims currently in the deck that this register does **not** yet support:

1. **Card 01** — Canyon Ranch sawmill opening July 1915 and the 4.5-mile
   distance to the dam site. [SFPUC-2005] p. 32 confirms the sawmill and its
   move to Hog Ranch, but not the opening date or the distance.
2. **Card 17** — the date 17 July 1931 for the Mitchell Shaft explosion.
   [SFPUC-2005] confirms twelve lives lost; the exact date is not in the text
   read here.
3. **Card 19** — "February 1934" for Alameda Creek Siphon No. 1. The
   publication's chronology table appears to give a day as well, but the table
   is multi-column and extracted with its year column displaced, so no date
   should be read from it without opening the page.
4. **Card 21** — the time "10:12 a.m." for the first water at Pulgas.
   [SFPUC-2005] p. 42 covers both October 1934 dates but does not give a time.
5. **Card 03** — the excursion card treats passenger service as a pure cost.
   [SFPUC-2005] p. 32 records that passengers paid 7½ cents per mile, and
   external research reports a 12.5-cents-per-ton-mile freight tariff under
   common-carrier operation from July 1918 to February 1925. Whether the card's
   `funds` cost should be reconsidered is a design question for the historian's
   answer to feed.

Items 1–4 are citation gaps: the claim may well be correct and simply needs its
page. Item 5 is a possible content change.
