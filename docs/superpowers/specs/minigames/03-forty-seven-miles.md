# Slot 3 — Forty-Seven Miles

**Status:** PLACEHOLDER. Not ready for implementation.
**Archetype:** Pipe Dream / Pipe Mania
**Cadence:** Recurring
**Priority:** After slot 1.

## Already decided — do not revisit

- **It covers divisions 4 and 6** — the San Joaquin valley pipeline and the bay
  crossing. These have no tunnelling, so without this slot half the map reverts
  to clicking End Season. That is the structural reason it exists.
- **Connect the line before the water arrives.** Spatial planning under time
  pressure — the one slot with a clock.
- **Outputs:** miles, funds, readiness.
- **Placed pipe is overwritable**, chosen for forgiveness. Friction is the enemy
  on a showcase piece.

## Historical anchors

- [Forty-Seven Miles of Steel](../../../../content/cards/13_forty_seven_miles_of_steel.md)
  — the steel pipe run across the valley floor.
- [Leave Room for Four Pipes](../../../../content/cards/12_room_for_four_pipes.md)
  — the right-of-way staked wide enough for future capacity. **This is the most
  interesting historical idea available to this slot** and is currently unused
  by the mechanic: building for capacity you do not yet need.
- [Under the San Joaquin](../../../../content/cards/14_under_the_san_joaquin.md)
  — pipe on timber piles in a wet river-crossing trench.
- [Across Sunol Valley](../../../../content/cards/19_across_sunol_valley.md) —
  the Alameda Creek inverted siphon.

## Must be decided

1. **Grid size, and how the flow timer scales to it.** The timer is the whole
   difficulty curve; it must be derived from grid size rather than hand-set per
   level.
2. **Whether "room for four pipes" becomes a mechanic.** The obvious version:
   laying a wider right-of-way costs time and funds now but earns readiness — a
   direct expression of building for the future. This is the strongest
   historical hook in the slot and should be seriously considered rather than
   left as flavour text.
3. **Terrain variation across the two divisions.** Valley (fast, open) versus
   bay mud and the river crossing (obstacles, forced routing)? Some variation is
   needed or divisions 4 and 6 play identically.
4. **What "failure" is.** Water reaching an unconnected end — and then what? It
   must not be a hard loss; most likely partial credit for pipe laid.
5. **Tier thresholds**, and whether efficiency (pipe used versus minimum
   possible) feeds readiness separately from raw completion.

## Notes

Pipe Dream is the most-cloned puzzle on this slate and the implementation is
well-trodden — rotating tile grid, flow tick, connection check. The design work
here is almost entirely in questions 2 and 3, not in the core mechanic.
